---
title: Incidents and Slack
description: What happens when a monitor fires.
---

What happens when a monitor fires depends on whether it is armed.

## Unarmed monitors

When an unarmed monitor fires, OnePatch records the firing — its state, reason, and time — as telemetry you can query and chart. No one is paged and no incident is opened.

## Armed monitors

When an armed monitor fires, OnePatch opens an **incident** and the agent triages it:

1. It investigates the firing against your live telemetry.
2. It tunes the monitor if the firing was a false alarm, or opens a pull request against your repository if the problem is real.

An incident is itself a chat: you can watch the investigation as it happens, step in, and review any pull request before it merges. Arming is opt-in per monitor; arm one only when a firing warrants an automated response.

<Note>
A firing episode opens a single incident, however many evaluations fire during it, with a cooldown after it resolves.
</Note>

## Slack

Incident alerts post to the `#onepatch-alerts` channel OnePatch creates in your connected Slack workspace: which monitor fired, why, and a link into the incident chat. The channel name, workspace, and connection are managed from the integrations screen.

## Talking to the agent from Slack

Every conversation is the same agent the in-app chat uses. Slack conversations appear in the app's sidebar with a Slack marker and the sender's name, so they stay visible to the rest of your team.

Four ways to reach it:

- **Direct message.** Message the OnePatch bot and it answers as the in-app chat does. The DM is one continuing conversation.
- **@-mention in a channel.** Mention the bot in a channel it has been invited to and it replies in a thread. Each thread is its own conversation; reply in the thread to keep it going.
- **Reply in an incident thread.** Under an incident card in `#onepatch-alerts`, a reply joins that incident's chat. No mention needed.
- **Watched channels.** Point OnePatch at a channel — a bug-report or support channel, say — and it reads every new top-level message there without a mention, opening a thread on the ones that need attention.

<Note>
The bot only sees channels it has been invited to. `/invite @onepatch` in Slack first; removing it from a channel always stops it reading there.
</Note>

## Watched channels

Watching is opt-in per channel, and the list is managed from the integrations screen or from inside the channel itself. Mention the bot with one of these commands as the whole message:

| Say | Effect |
|---|---|
| `@onepatch watch` | Start watching this channel. It confirms in the channel. |
| `@onepatch unwatch` | Stop watching. It still answers when mentioned. |
| `@onepatch watch status` | Whether this channel is watched. |

`watch this channel`, `start watching`, `stop watching`, and `are you watching` work as well. Anything longer is a message, not a command — `@onepatch watch out, the deploy is landing` is a question for the agent, and the channel's watch state stays as it was.

What a watched channel does:

- Every new top-level message from a person opens its own thread and chat. Replies in that thread continue the same conversation, so a report and its follow-up stay together.
- A message with a screenshot and a caption counts; edits, deletions, and bot posts do not.
- The agent stays quiet when a message doesn't need it. A channel that gets an answer to every "thanks!" is a channel people mute.
- Only public channels can be watched, and only a member of your OnePatch workspace can turn watching on or off. Everything the agent reads there is visible to everyone in the OnePatch workspace, so a private channel is refused; so is `#onepatch-alerts`, where the agent would be triaging its own cards.
- Up to 20 channels per workspace. As a brake on runaway channels, a channel opens at most 20 new chats an hour and the workspace 60; messages over that are dropped, and replies to open threads are never counted.

## Firing history

Every firing is recorded as telemetry, so you can chart it:

```text
Show me how often the error-rate monitor fired over the last week.
```
