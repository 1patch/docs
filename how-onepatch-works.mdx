---
title: How OnePatch works
description: Telemetry in, an agent over your data, monitors and dashboards out.
---

OnePatch has three parts: the telemetry you send, the agent that reads it, and the monitors and dashboards the agent produces.

## Telemetry in

Your application sends OpenTelemetry — traces, metrics, and logs — to your workspace's ingest endpoint. You point your exporter at it once; OnePatch stores every signal and makes it queryable. This is the only manual setup. See [Getting started](/getting-started).

## The agent over your data

Your workspace runs an AI **agent** with read access to your telemetry and the repositories you connect. You interact with it in chat — the single interface to the product. Ask a question and the agent queries your telemetry and answers from the data. Ask it to watch or visualize something and it builds the monitor or dashboard.

## Monitors and dashboards out

The agent produces two things that outlive any chat:

- A **dashboard** is a live view of your telemetry.
- A **monitor** watches your telemetry and reports `healthy`, `firing`, or `unknown`.

Both are live as soon as the agent finishes writing them. [Monitors and dashboards](/monitors-and-dashboards) covers both in detail.

## When something breaks

A monitor can be **armed**. When an armed monitor fires, OnePatch opens an incident and the agent triages it: it investigates the firing, then tunes the monitor if it was a false alarm or opens a pull request if the problem is real. Notifications land in your `#onepatch-alerts` Slack channel. See [Incidents and Slack](/incidents-and-slack).

## When you ship

Every pull request on a connected repository gets a verification plan — checks against your telemetry for what the change should and should not do — posted as one review on the pull request. After it merges and deploys, the checks run through a soak; a regression opens an incident, and a clean result is one edited line on the pull request. See [Change verification](/change-verification).
